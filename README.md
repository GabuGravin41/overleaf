### Section 3: Econometrics & STEM Education Policy
*Location: [`03_economics_and_stem_policy/`](03_economics_and_stem_policy/)*

1. **[Economics, Institutional Architecture, and Educational Outcomes](03_economics_and_stem_policy/economics_stem_talent_imo_ioi_kenya_paper.md)**
   - **Subtitle**: *An Empirical and Econometric Investigation of Global STEM Talent Pipelines (IMO/IOI 2010–2025) and Sub-Regional Equity in Kenya*
   - **Formats**: Markdown (`economics_stem_talent_imo_ioi_kenya_paper.md`, 17.4 KB).
   - **Key Empirical Laws Established**:
     - *90% Institutional Alpha*: Country fixed effects explain 89.8% of cross-national STEM variance ($R^2 = 0.8981$), whereas short-term GDP fluctuations show zero statistical significance ($p = 0.765$).
     - *Endogenous Growth Loop*: Baseline 2010 IMO scores predict long-run GDP per capita growth ($\beta = +0.140, p = 0.076$).
     - *Education Spending Paradox*: Public expenditure as % of GDP shows a negative coefficient ($\beta = -2.64, p = 0.003$) without meritocratic talent structures.
     - *Household Mediation Law*: Controlling for household poverty ($p = 0.001$) and domestic electrification ($p = 0.0001$) collapses county macro-GCP influence to zero ($p = 0.895$).
